Dataset sourcing and evaluation for machine learning teams.

Parralax finds candidate datasets for a given task, reads their licenses, and tests them against what your model needs. You get a written assessment of what each dataset can and cannot support.

Describe your project What we do

Services

We find, vet and prepare the data a model is trained and tested on.

Dataset sourcing

Given a task and its constraints, we search public, academic and commercial sources and return candidates with notes on size, modality, labeling method and known issues.

Licensing and acquisition support

We read the terms attached to each dataset and summarize restrictions on commercial use, redistribution and derived models. We can also help draft questions for data providers. We are not lawyers, so the summary is meant for you or your counsel to review.

Dataset quality assessment

We sample and test for duplicates, label errors, class balance, overlap between training and test splits, and how closely the data matches your deployment conditions. You receive a written report with examples.

Data preparation

Cleaning, deduplication, format conversion and split design, delivered as documented Python scripts you can rerun. The output loads directly into a PyTorch pipeline.

Technical consulting

Advice on baselines, evaluation design and model choice for computer vision and transformer-based work, including when collecting different data is a better use of time than changing the model.

Approach

Dataset problems usually surface after training, when they are expensive to trace. We move that work to the start.

  1. Requirements first. We write down the task, the input conditions, the labels needed and the metric before looking at any data.
  2. Provenance and terms. Each candidate gets a record of where it came from and what its license allows.
  3. Look at the data. We read samples by hand and run the checks below. Dataset cards and paper abstracts are claims to verify.
  4. State the limits. The report says what was tested, what was not, and where we are unsure.
  5. Prefer the simple option. If a cleaned public dataset and a standard baseline are enough, that is the recommendation.
